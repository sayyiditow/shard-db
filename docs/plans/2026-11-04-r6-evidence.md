Zero AddressSanitizer/LeakSanitizer reports in all three runs (`ERROR:
AddressSanitizer` count = 0). The failures are three cases, root-caused below.

### Finding ASAN-1 (deterministic, 3/3 runs): auto-reshard wait budgets

`test-auto-reshard` (2 failures) and `test-auto-reshard-throttle` (2 failures):
`grown reshaped … within wait budget`, `both grown_a and grown_b … deadline`,
`huge … target=2048 within wait budget`, `found at least 2 AUTO-RESHARD done log lines`.

Root cause: the sweep is serialized, and under ASan on this machine a
1,050,000-record bulk + full kf/segment rebuild exceeds even the 6×
sanitizer-scaled budgets (`RESHARD_WAIT_SCALE 6` → 120 s single / 240 s
throttle). The file's own header doctrine: "None of this is a functional
problem — the reshard still runs and still reaches the right target, just
slower. Scale the *wait*, not the assertion." Post-budget correctness
assertions pass (count preserved). Timing calibration gap, not a product bug.
Calibration measurement: see below.

### Finding ASAN-2 (flaky, 1/3 runs): test-null-migration-e-marker-order

29 failures in run 1 only (count ≠ 45; 28 of 40 replayed-row finds missing);
passed in runs 2 and 3 with the identical binary.

Root cause (traced through `durability.c:198 durability_test_pause` and
`slotcask.c:6043 bulk_publish_window_marker_locked` / `slotcask.c:6276 win-I`):
durable commit markers are per-kf-shard files (`<shard>_batch_<batch>_marker.dat`,
published at win-M, before win-I of the same chain); the pause marker is a
single shared filename (`.durability-test-win-I.active`) written by whichever
chain reaches win-I first. The test kills on the FIRST chain's marker — under
sanitizer scheduling skew, other chains may not yet have published their
markers; those windows are then legitimately lost (marker = commit point;
recovery is forward-replay-only). Fast builds march in lockstep, so the
assumption holds there. Test-design timing assumption, not a product bug —
the engine behaved exactly as the durability doctrine requires.
